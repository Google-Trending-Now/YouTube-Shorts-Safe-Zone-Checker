Why Use a Shorts UI Overlay Preview Tool?

  • Keep your 3-second hook readable: If your opening text sits too high or too low, YouTube's search bar or description cuts it off—and viewers swipe away immediately.
  • Stop subtitles from colliding with the Subscribe button: Auto-captions in CapCut and Premiere often default to the lower third, right where the channel name and audio ticker live.
  • Protect product demos and sponsor tags: The right-side column takes up 20% of the frame width. Checking your edit with a shorts UI overlay preview tool ensures key visuals stay clear of the Like and Comment icons.
  • Export once instead of re-uploading: Spot layout problems in 10 seconds inside your browser instead of publishing an unlisted test Short to your phone.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the exact YouTube Shorts safe zone dimensions in pixels?

On a standard 1080×1920px vertical canvas, leave 288px (15%) clear at the top, 672px (35%) at the bottom, 216px (20%) on the right, and at least 48px on the left. That gives you a reliable 816×960px safe box in the center-left for captions, hooks, and visual callouts.

Does this YouTube Shorts safe zone checker upload my video files?

No. Every image or video you load stays strictly on your device using your browser's local memory. Nothing is sent to a server, so unreleased client edits and sponsor reads stay completely private.

How do I use the downloaded PNG template in Premiere Pro, CapCut, or DaVinci Resolve?

Click the Download PNG Overlay Template button and drag the file onto the top video track of your 9:16 timeline. Align your subtitles and graphics inside the green dashed rectangle, then mute or delete the guide track right before exporting.

Why do I need a shorts UI overlay preview tool if my editor already has title-safe guides?

Standard broadcast title-safe margins are built for horizontal TV screens and ignore mobile app buttons. A shorts UI overlay preview tool mirrors YouTube's actual Like, Comment, Share, Subscribe, and caption placements so your hook never gets buried behind the interface.
